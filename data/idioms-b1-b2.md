# Idioms y expresiones frecuentes B1--B2

Selección de expresiones relativamente comunes y útiles. Conviene aprender la situación en la que se usan, no solo la traducción.

- **a piece of cake** --- very easy --- muy fácil --- *The test was a piece of cake.* --- *El examen fue muy fácil.* --- *The homework was a piece of cake. I finished it in ten minutes. Let's play football now.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **break the ice** --- make people feel more relaxed when they first meet --- romper el hielo --- *A joke can help break the ice.* --- *Un chiste puede ayudar a romper el hielo.* --- *Jo’s funny hat deserved a vote of thanks, for it was of general utility. It broke the ice in the beginning by producing a laugh, it created quite a refreshing breeze, flapping to and fro as she rowed, and would make an excellent umbrella for the whole party, if a shower came up, she said.* --- *Little Women — Louisa May Alcott* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **call it a day** --- stop working for the day --- dar por terminado por hoy --- *I'm tired. Let's call it a day.* --- *Estoy cansado. Demos por terminado el día.* --- *We painted two rooms already. Let's call it a day. We continue tomorrow morning.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **cost an arm and a leg** --- be very expensive --- costar una fortuna --- *That car costs an arm and a leg.* --- *Ese auto cuesta una fortuna.* --- *My new glasses cost an arm and a leg. I need them for driving. At least they look good.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **get out of hand** --- become impossible to control --- salirse de control --- *The situation got out of hand.* --- *La situación se salió de control.* --- *The party got out of hand fast. Music was loud until three. Neighbors called twice.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **give someone a hand** --- help someone --- ayudar a alguien --- *Can you give me a hand?* --- *¿Me podés dar una mano?* --- *These bags are heavy. Please give me a hand. The stairs are too many.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **hang in there** --- continue despite difficulties --- aguantar/no rendirse --- *Hang in there. You're almost finished.* --- *Aguantá. Ya casi terminás.* --- *The exam is almost over. Hang in there for ten minutes. You studied hard all week.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **hit the nail on the head** --- say exactly the right thing --- dar en el clavo --- *You hit the nail on the head.* --- *Diste en el clavo.* --- *Your guess hit the nail on the head. The answer was Lima. How did you know?* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **in the same boat** --- in the same difficult situation --- estar en la misma situación --- *We're all in the same boat.* --- *Todos estamos en la misma situación.* --- *We are all in the same boat with this test. Nobody understands chapter five. Let's study together tonight.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **keep an eye on** --- watch carefully --- vigilar --- *Can you keep an eye on my bag?* --- *¿Podés vigilar mi bolso?* --- *It seems a shame to have neglected No. 14 all this time; and yet we may be sure that Mrs. Darling does not blame us. If we had returned sooner to look with sorrowful sympathy at her, she would probably have cried, “Don’t be silly; what do I matter? Do go back and keep an eye on the children.”* --- *Peter Pan — J. M. Barrie*
- **learn the hard way** --- learn through an unpleasant experience --- aprender por las malas --- *I learned that lesson the hard way.* --- *Aprendí esa lección por las malas.* --- *He touched the hot pan and learned the hard way. Now he always uses gloves. His finger still hurts.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **miss the point** --- fail to understand the main idea --- no entender el punto --- *I think you're missing the point.* --- *Creo que no estás entendiendo el punto.* --- *You missed the point of my story. It was about helping, not money. Listen to the end this time.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **on the right track** --- thinking or acting in a way likely to succeed --- ir por buen camino --- *Your pronunciation is on the right track.* --- *Tu pronunciación va por buen camino.* --- *Your English is on the right track. Your verbs are much better. Keep practicing every day.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **out of the blue** --- unexpectedly --- inesperadamente --- *She called me out of the blue.* --- *Me llamó inesperadamente.* --- *Marco called me out of the blue. We lost contact years ago. Now we meet every month.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **pull someone's leg** --- joke with someone by trying to make them believe something false --- tomarle el pelo a alguien --- *Relax, I'm just pulling your leg.* --- *Relajate, solo te estoy tomando el pelo.* --- *Don't worry, I am just pulling your leg. Your hair looks fine. We will arrive on time.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **see eye to eye** --- agree completely --- estar de acuerdo --- *We don't always see eye to eye.* --- *No siempre estamos de acuerdo.* --- *We rarely see eye to eye on films. He loves horror and I love comedy. So we take turns choosing.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **so far, so good** --- things are going well until now --- hasta ahora, todo bien --- *How's the project? So far, so good.* --- *¿Cómo va el proyecto? Hasta ahora, todo bien.* --- *How's the trip? So far, so good. The hotel is clean and cheap. Tomorrow we visit the old town.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **speak of the devil** --- said when the person being discussed appears --- hablando del rey de Roma --- *Speak of the devil! We were just talking about you.* --- *Hablando del rey de Roma. Justo estábamos hablando de vos.* --- *Speak of the devil! We were talking about your trip. Come and tell us everything.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **take your time** --- do not hurry --- tomate tu tiempo --- *Take your time. There's no hurry.* --- *Tomate tu tiempo. No hay apuro.* --- *Take your time with the form. There are no wrong answers. I will wait outside.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **the last straw** --- the final problem that makes a situation unbearable --- la gota que colmó el vaso --- *That mistake was the last straw.* --- *Ese error fue la gota que colmó el vaso.* --- *The broken printer was the last straw. We lost a full day. The boss bought a new one.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **under the weather** --- feeling slightly ill --- sentirse indispuesto --- *I'm feeling a little under the weather.* --- *Me siento un poco indispuesto.* --- *You look pale today. Are you feeling under the weather? Sit down and drink some water.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **up in the air** --- not decided yet --- sin decidir --- *Our travel plans are still up in the air.* --- *Nuestros planes de viaje todavía no están decididos.* --- *Our holiday plans are still up in the air. Dad wants the beach, Mom wants the city. We vote on Sunday.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **better late than never** --- it is better to do something late than not at all --- mejor tarde que nunca --- *You finally started exercising---better late than never.* --- *Por fin empezaste a hacer ejercicio: mejor tarde que nunca.* --- *You finally called, better late than never. I waited all afternoon. Tell me your news now.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **easier said than done** --- more difficult to do than to talk about --- más fácil decirlo que hacerlo --- *Changing habits is easier said than done.* --- *Cambiar hábitos es más fácil decirlo que hacerlo.* --- *Waking at five is easier said than done. My bed feels too good. I need three alarms.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **once in a blue moon** --- very rarely --- muy de vez en cuando --- *I eat fast food once in a blue moon.* --- *Como comida rápida muy de vez en cuando.* --- *We eat pizza once in a blue moon. Mom says it is not healthy. But tonight is special.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **get the hang of it** --- learn how to do something --- agarrarle la mano --- *You'll get the hang of it with practice.* --- *Le vas a agarrar la mano con práctica.* --- *Skiing is hard at first. You will get the hang of it soon. I fell ten times yesterday.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **go the extra mile** --- make more effort than expected --- hacer un esfuerzo adicional --- *She always goes the extra mile for her clients.* --- *Siempre hace un esfuerzo adicional por sus clientes.* --- *Our teacher goes the extra mile for us. She stays late every Thursday. We all passed thanks to her.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **make up your mind** --- make a decision or accept something as certain --- decidirse / hacerse a la idea --- *You need to make up your mind.* --- *Tenés que decidirte.* --- *“You’ll stay in this room until you confess, Anne. You can make up your mind to that,” she said firmly. “But the picnic is tomorrow, Marilla,” cried Anne.* --- *Anne of Green Gables — Lucy Maud Montgomery*
- **no big deal** --- not important or serious --- no es para tanto --- *Don't worry. It's no big deal.* --- *No te preocupes. No es para tanto.* --- *“I broke your pen, sorry.” “It's no big deal; I have another. Don't think about it.”* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **on the same page** --- share the same understanding or opinion --- estar de acuerdo/entender lo mismo --- *Let's make sure we're on the same page.* --- *Asegurémonos de que entendemos lo mismo.* --- *Before we start, let's get on the same page. Who brings food and who brings drinks? Write it here.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **ring a bell** --- seem familiar --- resultar familiar --- *That name rings a bell.* --- *Ese nombre me resulta familiar.* --- *That song rings a bell. Did we hear it last summer? It played everywhere on the beach.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **take it easy** --- relax and do not worry --- tomárselo con calma --- *Take it easy this weekend.* --- *Tomátelo con calma este fin de semana.* --- *Don't run today. Take it easy and rest. Your ankle needs one calm day.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **think outside the box** --- think creatively --- pensar de manera creativa --- *We need to think outside the box.* --- *Tenemos que pensar de manera creativa.* --- *We need a gift for Dad. Let's think outside the box. He hates normal presents.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **time flies** --- time passes quickly --- el tiempo vuela --- *Time flies when you're having fun.* --- *El tiempo vuela cuando te divertís.* --- *Time flies at Grandma's house. We play cards all afternoon. Suddenly it is dark outside.* --- *Everyday conversation*
- **worth a shot** --- worth trying --- vale la pena intentarlo --- *I'm not sure it'll work, but it's worth a shot.* --- *No estoy seguro de que funcione, pero vale la pena intentarlo.* --- *I never fixed a bike, but it's worth a shot. Bring your tools on Saturday. We learn together.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **get cold feet** --- become too nervous to do something planned --- acobardarse a último momento --- *I got cold feet before the interview.* --- *Me acobardé antes de la entrevista.* --- *At the station, Leo got cold feet. He held the ticket in his hand, watching the train as its doors began to close.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **give it a try** --- attempt something to see if it works --- intentarlo --- *Give it a try; you might enjoy it.* --- *Intentá hacerlo; quizá te guste.* --- *The old bicycle looked impossible to repair. Still, Lina decided to give it a try before taking the long walk home.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **keep your fingers crossed** --- hope that something happens successfully --- cruzar los dedos --- *Keep your fingers crossed for me.* --- *Cruzá los dedos por mí.* --- *As the results were announced, Tom asked his sister to keep her fingers crossed. He had never wanted a place at the school so badly.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **have second thoughts** --- begin to doubt a decision --- tener dudas / reconsiderar --- *I'm having second thoughts about the trip.* --- *Estoy reconsiderando el viaje.* --- *At the harbour, Ada had second thoughts about leaving. The little house on the hill looked smaller than she remembered.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **make ends meet** --- have enough money for basic needs --- llegar a fin de mes --- *They struggled to make ends meet.* --- *Les costaba llegar a fin de mes.* --- *After the shop closed, it was hard to make ends meet. Each Sunday, her father counted the coins beside the kitchen window.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **be in two minds** --- be unable to decide between two choices --- estar indeciso --- *I'm in two minds about the offer.* --- *Estoy indeciso con la propuesta.* --- *Nora was in two minds about the offer. The new job meant more money, but it would take her far from home.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **on purpose** --- intentionally, not by mistake --- a propósito --- *Did you leave the door open on purpose?* --- *¿Dejaste la puerta abierta a propósito?* --- *The window had been left open on purpose. Clara knew it the moment she saw the dry floor beneath it, untouched by the rain.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **by accident** --- without intending to --- por accidente / sin querer --- *I found the letter by accident.* --- *Encontré la carta sin querer.* --- *By accident, Ben opened the wrong drawer and found a photograph of the house as it had looked fifty years before.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **in a hurry** --- needing to do something quickly --- apurado --- *I'm in a hurry; I'll call you later.* --- *Estoy apurado; te llamo después.* --- *She was in a hurry when she left the inn. Only at the bridge did she notice that the little red book was still on the table.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **at least** --- no less than; used to point out a positive side --- al menos / por lo menos --- *At least we arrived before the rain.* --- *Por lo menos llegamos antes de la lluvia.* --- *The road was longer than they had expected. At least the rain had stopped, and they could see the village below.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **in the long run** --- over a long period of time --- a la larga --- *It will save money in the long run.* --- *A la larga va a ahorrar plata.* --- *Repairing the old roof cost them more than they had hoped. In the long run, though, it kept the house warm and dry for years.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **come in handy** --- be useful at a particular moment --- venir bien / resultar útil --- *This map might come in handy.* --- *Este mapa podría venir bien.* --- *Nora almost threw away the small torch. It came in handy that evening, when the lights went out halfway up the stairs.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **have a lot on your plate** --- have many things to deal with --- tener muchas cosas entre manos --- *I have a lot on my plate this week.* --- *Tengo muchas cosas entre manos esta semana.* --- *With the shop to run and her father to look after, Eva had a lot on her plate. Still, she made time to visit the garden each morning.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken & written*
- **give someone a heads-up** --- warn or tell someone about something in advance --- avisarle a alguien con tiempo --- *Thanks for giving me a heads-up.* --- *Gracias por avisarme con tiempo.* --- *Before the guests arrived, Sam gave his sister a heads-up about the broken step. She placed a lantern beside it and waited by the door.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **get straight to the point** --- say the most important thing without delay --- ir directo al grano --- *Let's get straight to the point.* --- *Vayamos directo al grano.* --- *The doctor sat down and got straight to the point. The boy would recover, but he would need a long rest away from the sea.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **be on the safe side** --- take extra care to avoid a problem --- por las dudas / para estar seguro --- *Take an umbrella to be on the safe side.* --- *Llevá un paraguas por las dudas.* --- *To be on the safe side, Mila copied the address into her notebook. By morning, the writing on the original paper had faded.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **make the most of** --- use an opportunity as well as possible --- aprovechar al máximo --- *Let's make the most of the weekend.* --- *Aprovechemos al máximo el fin de semana.* --- *They had only one day in the city, so they made the most of it. By sunset, they had walked from the market to the old harbour.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **take something for granted** --- fail to appreciate something because it seems certain --- dar algo por sentado --- *Don't take your friends for granted.* --- *No des por sentados a tus amigos.* --- *For years, Leo took the quiet evenings at home for granted. In the crowded city, he missed the sound of rain on their roof.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **go with the flow** --- accept changes instead of trying to control everything --- dejarse llevar --- *We had no plan and just went with the flow.* --- *No teníamos un plan y nos dejamos llevar.* --- *When the train stopped in an unfamiliar town, they decided to go with the flow. A sign led them to a little fair beside the river.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **lose track of time** --- stop noticing how much time has passed --- perder la noción del tiempo --- *I lost track of time while reading.* --- *Perdí la noción del tiempo mientras leía.* --- *Ada lost track of time among the books. When she finally looked up, the library windows were dark and the last visitor had gone.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
- **be up to someone** --- be someone's responsibility or decision --- depender de alguien / quedar en manos de alguien --- *It's up to you where we go.* --- *Depende de vos adónde vamos.* --- *It was up to Rosa to choose which path they would take. She studied the map once more, then pointed towards the hills.* --- *Fragmento narrativo original*
