## Present Simple / Present Progressive
### Regla y forma
- **EN:** The **present simple** uses the base verb and adds **-s/-es** in the third person; questions and negatives rely on **do/does**. The **present progressive** combines **am/is/are + the -ing form**; in questions the subject and the auxiliary swap places.
- **ES:** El **present simple** usa el verbo base y agrega **-s/-es** en tercera persona; para preguntas y negativas se apoya en **do/does**. El **present progressive** combina **am/is/are + verbo en -ing**; en preguntas se invierte el orden del sujeto y el auxiliar.
### Formación
- **Name (EN):** Present Simple
  - **Affirmative:** subject + base verb (+ -s/-es in the third person)
  - **Negative:** subject + don't/doesn't + base verb
  - **Question:** Do/does + subject + base verb?
- **Name (EN):** Present Progressive
  - **Affirmative:** subject + am/is/are + -ing form
  - **Negative:** subject + am/is/are + not + -ing form
  - **Question:** Am/is/are + subject + -ing form?
### Definición
- **EN:** The **present simple** describes what is generally true or happens regularly; it treats an activity as a fact or habit, without showing it in progress. The **present progressive** presents an activity as unfolding around the moment of speaking or within a temporary period. The difference is perspective, not simply whether the event happens today: I work here describes a usual situation; I am working here this month describes a temporary one.
- **ES:** El **present simple** describe lo que es cierto en general o sucede habitualmente; presenta una actividad como hecho o costumbre, sin mostrarla en desarrollo. El **present progressive** muestra una actividad que transcurre al hablar o durante un período temporal. La diferencia es la perspectiva, no simplemente si ocurre hoy: I work here describe una situación habitual; I am working here this month, una temporal.
### Usos
- **EN:** Use the present simple for habits, general facts and fixed timetables. Use the present progressive for actions happening now, temporary situations and personal plans already arranged. Expressions like every day point to the simple; now, at the moment and this week usually point to the progressive.
- **ES:** Usá present simple para hábitos, hechos generales y horarios establecidos. Usá present progressive para acciones que ocurren ahora, situaciones temporales y planes personales ya organizados. Expresiones como every day orientan al simple; now, at the moment y this week suelen orientar al progressive.
### Contrastes y errores
- **EN:** A repeated habit does not take -ing just because it happens in the present. In the third person, She work is wrong: the simple needs works. After does, the verb goes back to the base form: Does she work?, not Does she works?. For a temporary situation, I am staying with friends conveys a limited duration, not a permanent habit.
- **ES:** Un hábito repetido no requiere -ing solo porque ocurre en el presente. En tercera persona, She work es incorrecto: el simple exige works. Después de does, el verbo vuelve a la base: Does she work?, no Does she works? Para una situación temporal, I am staying with friends comunica una duración limitada, no una costumbre permanente.
### Ejemplos
- **EN:** My brother takes the train every morning. --- **ES:** Mi hermano toma el tren todas las mañanas.
- **EN:** I am staying with my aunt this month. --- **ES:** Este mes me estoy quedando en lo de mi tía.
- **EN:** The museum opens at nine on weekdays. --- **ES:** El museo abre a las nueve los días de semana.
### Ejercicios
- **Prompt (EN):** Your neighbor walks to work every day. Say this as a regular habit using she and walk. --- **Model (EN):** She walks to work every day. --- **Explanation (ES):** Un hábito lleva *present simple* y la tercera persona singular agrega *-s*. --- **Translation (ES):** Ella camina al trabajo todos los días.
- **Prompt (EN):** You are on the phone and your brother is cooking right now. Describe what he is doing. --- **Model (EN):** My brother is cooking dinner right now. --- **Explanation (ES):** Una acción en desarrollo ahora lleva *is* más el verbo con *-ing*. --- **Translation (ES):** Mi hermano está preparando la cena ahora mismo.
- **Prompt (EN):** Ask a classmate whether Sara usually studies in the library. Use she in your present simple question. --- **Model (EN):** Does she usually study in the library? --- **Explanation (ES):** Con *does* en una pregunta, el verbo principal queda en forma base: *study*. --- **Translation (ES):** ¿Ella suele estudiar en la biblioteca?
- **Prompt (EN):** This week you have a temporary job at a café. Tell a friend what you are doing this week. --- **Model (EN):** I am working at a café this week. --- **Explanation (ES):** Una situación limitada a esta semana se expresa con *present progressive*. --- **Translation (ES):** Esta semana estoy trabajando en un café.
- **Prompt (EN):** The last bus departs at 11 p.m. every night. State its schedule. --- **Model (EN):** The last bus leaves at 11 p.m. every night. --- **Explanation (ES):** Un horario fijo se expresa con *present simple*, aunque se refiera a una salida futura. --- **Translation (ES):** El último colectivo sale a las once de la noche todos los días.

## Stative verbs
### Regla y forma
- **EN:** **Stative verbs** describe states and usually take the **present simple**. Some of them can also describe temporary actions; in that use they accept **am/is/are + the -ing form**.
- **ES:** Los **stative verbs** describen estados y suelen ir en **present simple**. Algunos verbos también pueden describir acciones temporales; en ese uso aceptan **am/is/are + verbo en -ing**.
### Formación
- **Name (EN):** Stative verbs (state)
  - **Affirmative:** subject + stative verb in the present simple
  - **Negative:** subject + don't/doesn't + stative verb in the base form
  - **Question:** Do/does + subject + stative verb in the base form?
- **Name (EN):** Temporary activity
  - **Affirmative:** subject + am/is/are + -ing form
  - **Negative:** subject + am/is/are + not + -ing form
  - **Question:** Am/is/are + subject + -ing form?
### Definición
- **EN:** **Stative verbs** describe a condition, perception, possession or mental state rather than an action taking place: know means having knowledge, and own means possessing something. Such meanings normally use the simple form even when they are true right now. Some verbs also have an action meaning: think can mean hold an opinion (state) or actively consider something (activity). Only the activity meaning naturally takes the progressive.
- **ES:** Los **stative verbs** describen un estado, una percepción, una posesión o una situación mental, no una acción en desarrollo: know significa saber y own significa poseer. Con esos sentidos suelen ir en forma simple aunque sean ciertos ahora mismo. Algunos verbos también tienen un sentido de acción: think puede significar opinar (estado) o considerar algo activamente (actividad). Solo el sentido de actividad admite naturalmente el progressive.
### Usos
- **EN:** Use the present simple for beliefs, knowledge, possession and stable relationships: know a person, own a bicycle, belong to a team. For a deliberate activity that is going on, think about, have a meal and taste a dish can appear in the progressive. The concrete meaning decides the form, not just the list of verbs.
- **ES:** Usá present simple para creencias, conocimiento, posesión y relaciones estables: know a person, own a bicycle, belong to a team. Para una actividad deliberada que está ocurriendo, think about, have a meal y taste a dish pueden aparecer en progressive. El significado concreto decide la forma, no solamente la lista de verbos.
### Contrastes y errores
- **EN:** I am knowing the answer is wrong to express knowledge; say I know the answer. I think this is useful states an opinion; I am thinking about it describes the process of considering it. Have as possession usually takes the simple; have as experience or meals allows the progressive.
- **ES:** I am knowing the answer es incorrecto para expresar conocimiento; decí I know the answer. I think this is useful expresa una opinión; I am thinking about it describe el proceso de considerarlo. Have como posesión suele ser simple; have como experiencia o comida admite progressive.
### Ejemplos
- **EN:** These keys belong to our neighbor. --- **ES:** Estas llaves pertenecen a nuestro vecino.
- **EN:** I think the new schedule is practical. --- **ES:** Creo que el nuevo horario es práctico.
- **EN:** The chef is tasting the soup before serving it. --- **ES:** El cocinero está probando la sopa antes de servirla.
### Ejercicios
- **Prompt (EN):** You recognize the answer to a question. Tell your classmate using know in a natural form. --- **Model (EN):** I know the answer to that question. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Know* expresa conocimiento como estado y normalmente va en *present simple*. --- **Translation (ES):** Sé la respuesta a esa pregunta.
- **Prompt (EN):** A friend is holding your coat. Tell them that it belongs to you. --- **Model (EN):** That coat belongs to me. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Belong* indica una relación de posesión y no suele usarse en *progressive*. --- **Translation (ES):** Ese abrigo me pertenece.
- **Prompt (EN):** You are considering a new course this week. Describe the ongoing thought process with think about. --- **Model (EN):** I am thinking about taking a new course. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Think about* puede describir la actividad temporal de considerar una opción. --- **Translation (ES):** Estoy pensando en hacer un curso nuevo.
- **Prompt (EN):** You have an opinion about this film: it is too long. Express your opinion with think. --- **Model (EN):** I think this film is too long. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Think* como opinión expresa un estado y se usa normalmente en *simple*. --- **Translation (ES):** Creo que esta película es demasiado larga.
- **Prompt (EN):** Your friend is eating lunch at this moment. Describe the activity with have. --- **Model (EN):** My friend is having lunch right now. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Have* en la expresión *have lunch* describe una actividad y admite *progressive*. --- **Translation (ES):** Mi amigo está almorzando ahora mismo.

## Comparisons
### Regla y forma
- **EN:** To compare, short adjectives usually add **-er + than** and long ones use **more + adjective + than**. Equality is built with **as + adjective + as**; the superlative, with **the + -est** or **the most + adjective**.
- **ES:** Para comparar, los adjetivos cortos suelen sumar **-er + than** y los largos usan **more + adjetivo + than**. La igualdad se arma con **as + adjetivo + as**; el superlativo, con **the + -est** o **the most + adjetivo**.
### Formación
- **Name (EN):** Comparative --- **Pattern:** subject + be + short adjective + -er + than + second reference / subject + be + more + long adjective + than + second reference.
- **Name (EN):** Equality --- **Pattern:** subject + be + as + adjective + as + second reference.
- **Name (EN):** Superlative --- **Pattern:** subject + be + the + short adjective + -est (+ noun) / the most + long adjective (+ noun).
### Definición
- **EN:** **Comparisons** express how two or more things relate in degree. A **comparative** compares one thing with another (bigger than); **as ... as** expresses equal degree and **not as ... as** a lower degree. A **superlative** identifies the highest or lowest degree within a defined group (the biggest in the class). These are different comparisons, not interchangeable endings for the same sentence.
- **ES:** Las **comparisons** expresan cómo se relacionan dos o más cosas en cuanto a grado. El **comparative** compara una cosa con otra (bigger than); **as ... as** indica igualdad y **not as ... as**, un grado menor. El **superlative** identifica el grado máximo o mínimo dentro de un grupo definido (the biggest in the class). Son comparaciones distintas, no terminaciones intercambiables para una misma oración.
### Usos
- **EN:** Use the comparative for a difference between two things and the superlative to highlight an extreme among three or more. As ... as marks equality and not as ... as indicates a lower degree. Less ... than expresses a smaller amount or degree; to count concrete objects, fewer ... than is more precise.
- **ES:** Usá el comparativo para una diferencia entre dos cosas y el superlativo para destacar un extremo entre tres o más. As ... as señala igualdad y not as ... as indica menor grado. Less ... than expresa menor cantidad o grado; para contar objetos concretos, fewer ... than es más preciso.
### Contrastes y errores
- **EN:** Do not combine double markers: more easier and the most fastest are wrong. With a short adjective like small, write smaller than and the smallest. With a long one like interesting, use more interesting than and the most interesting. After than you mention the second reference, while a superlative can include in or of to define the group.
- **ES:** No combines marcas dobles: more easier y the most fastest son incorrectos. Con un adjetivo corto como small, escribí smaller than y the smallest. Con uno largo como interesting, usá more interesting than y the most interesting. Después de than se menciona el segundo referente, mientras que un superlativo puede incluir in u of para definir el grupo.
### Ejemplos
- **EN:** This route is shorter than the coastal road. --- **ES:** Esta ruta es más corta que el camino costero.
- **EN:** That was the most interesting lecture of the week. --- **ES:** Esa fue la charla más interesante de la semana.
- **EN:** My room is not as bright as yours. --- **ES:** Mi habitación no es tan luminosa como la tuya.
### Ejercicios
- **Prompt (EN):** Two jackets cost different amounts: the blue one costs less. Compare the blue jacket with the red one using cheap. --- **Model (EN):** The blue jacket is cheaper than the red one. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Cheap* es un adjetivo corto; el comparativo agrega *-er* y se une al segundo referente con *than*. --- **Translation (ES):** La campera azul es más barata que la roja.
- **Prompt (EN):** Of the three restaurants in your street, one serves the best food. Describe it using good. --- **Model (EN):** This is the best restaurant on my street. --- **Explanation (ES):** El superlativo irregular de *good* es *the best* cuando se destaca uno dentro de un grupo. --- **Translation (ES):** Este es el mejor restaurante de mi calle.
- **Prompt (EN):** Your first exam was difficult, but the second was more difficult. Compare them. --- **Model (EN):** The second exam was more difficult than the first. --- **Explanation (ES):** Con el adjetivo largo *difficult*, el comparativo se forma con *more* y *than*. --- **Translation (ES):** El segundo examen fue más difícil que el primero.
- **Prompt (EN):** Your bicycle and your friend's bicycle are equally fast. Express that equality with as. --- **Model (EN):** My bicycle is as fast as my friend's bicycle. --- **Explanation (ES):** La igualdad de grado se expresa con *as* más adjetivo más *as*. --- **Translation (ES):** Mi bicicleta es tan rápida como la de mi amigo.
- **Prompt (EN):** In your class, no one arrives earlier than Sara. Describe Sara using an early superlative. --- **Model (EN):** Sara is the earliest student in our class. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Early* cambia la letra *y* por *i* antes de *-est*; *the* marca el superlativo dentro del grupo. --- **Translation (ES):** Sara es la alumna que llega más temprano de nuestra clase.

## Countable and uncountable nouns
### Regla y forma
- **EN:** Countable nouns can be singular or plural; uncountable nouns usually take no plural and no a or an. Use **many/few** and **how many** with plural countables, and **much/little** and **how much** with uncountables.
- **ES:** Los contables pueden ir en singular o plural; los incontables no suelen llevar plural ni a o an. Usá **many/few** y **how many** con contables plurales, y **much/little** y **how much** con incontables.
### Formación
- **Name (EN):** Countable nouns --- **Pattern:** Many/few + plural countable noun. Question: how many + plural countable noun?
- **Name (EN):** Uncountable nouns --- **Pattern:** Much/little + uncountable noun. Question: how much + uncountable noun?
### Definición
- **EN:** A **countable noun** refers to separate units you can count: one chair, two chairs. An **uncountable noun** refers to something treated as a mass or an undivided concept: water, furniture, advice. This grammatical distinction determines whether you can use a/an, a plural and expressions such as many or much. It is about how English treats the noun, not whether you can measure the thing in real life: you can count pieces of furniture, but not furnitures in this sense.
- **ES:** Un **countable noun** nombra unidades separadas que podés contar: one chair, two chairs. Un **uncountable noun** nombra algo tratado como masa o concepto no dividido: water, furniture, advice. Esta distinción gramatical determina si podés usar a/an, plural y expresiones como many o much. Se trata de cómo el inglés clasifica el sustantivo, no de si en la vida real podés medirlo: podés contar pieces of furniture, pero no furnitures con ese sentido.
### Usos
- **EN:** Use many and few with plural countables; much and little with uncountables. Some and a lot of work for both types in the right contexts. To ask about quantity, How many goes with a plural countable noun and How much goes with an uncountable one.
- **ES:** Usá many y few con plurales contables; much y little con incontables. Some y a lot of sirven para ambos tipos en contextos adecuados. Para preguntar por cantidad, How many acompaña un plural contable y How much acompaña un incontable.
### Contrastes y errores
- **EN:** An information and two advices are wrong in this sense: say some information and two pieces of advice. Do not confuse the container with the content: two bottles of water counts bottles; it does not turn water into a plural. A few offers a small number of units; a little expresses a small amount of a substance.
- **ES:** An information y two advices son incorrectos en este sentido: decí some information y two pieces of advice. No confundas el recipiente con el contenido: two bottles of water cuenta botellas, no convierte a water en plural. A few ofrece una pequeña cantidad de unidades; a little expresa una pequeña cantidad de una sustancia.
### Ejemplos
- **EN:** We bought three oranges and some rice. --- **ES:** Compramos tres naranjas y algo de arroz.
- **EN:** How much luggage can you bring on the plane? --- **ES:** ¿Cuánto equipaje podés llevar en el avión?
- **EN:** She gave us two pieces of useful advice. --- **ES:** Ella nos dio dos consejos útiles.
### Ejercicios
- **Prompt (EN):** You need details about a course. Ask for information without using a or a plural ending. --- **Model (EN):** Could you give me some information about the course? --- **Explanation (ES):** *Information* es incontable; *some* sirve para pedir una cantidad no especificada. --- **Translation (ES):** ¿Podrías darme información sobre el curso?
- **Prompt (EN):** Your group needs chairs for a meeting. Ask a colleague how many chairs are available. --- **Model (EN):** How many chairs are available? --- **Explanation (ES):** *Chairs* es un plural contable y se pregunta por su número con *how many*. --- **Translation (ES):** ¿Cuántas sillas hay disponibles?
- **Prompt (EN):** You need water for a recipe. Ask about its quantity with how much. --- **Model (EN):** How much water do we need for the recipe? --- **Explanation (ES):** *Water* es incontable; *how much* pregunta por su cantidad. --- **Translation (ES):** ¿Cuánta agua necesitamos para la receta?
- **Prompt (EN):** A friend gave you three suggestions. Thank them for three pieces of advice. --- **Model (EN):** Thank you for those three pieces of advice. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Advice* es incontable; *pieces* permite contar tres unidades de consejo. --- **Translation (ES):** Gracias por esos tres consejos.
- **Prompt (EN):** Only a small amount of milk is left in the fridge. Describe it with a little. --- **Model (EN):** There is a little milk left in the fridge. --- **Explanation (ES):** *Milk* es incontable y *a little* indica una cantidad pequeña pero existente. --- **Translation (ES):** Queda un poco de leche en la heladera.
