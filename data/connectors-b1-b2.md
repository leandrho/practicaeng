# Connectors B1--B2

Conectores frecuentes para unir ideas, dar ejemplos y ordenar un texto o una conversación. Cada tarjeta agrupa el conector bajo la función para la que se usa.

## Adding information

- **moreover** --- in addition to what has been said --- y además, encima --- *The hotel was cheap; moreover, it was close to the centre.* --- *El hotel era barato; además, estaba cerca del centro.* --- *Marilla felt an uncomfortable conviction that, if she denied the appeal of that look, it would haunt her to her dying day. Moreover, she did not fancy Mrs. Blewett.* --- *Anne of Green Gables — Lucy Maud Montgomery* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **furthermore** --- in addition; used to add a stronger point --- además, y además --- *The room is small; furthermore, there is no Wi-Fi.* --- *La habitación es pequeña; además, no hay Wi-Fi.* --- *The hotel was noisy and far from the centre. Furthermore, the staff never answered the phone.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **in addition** --- besides; used to add information --- además, adicionalmente --- *The flat is cheap; in addition, it includes bills.* --- *El departamento es barato; además, incluye expensas.* --- *That trick of staining the fishes’ scales of a delicate pink is quite peculiar to China. When, in addition, I see a Chinese coin hanging from your watch-chain, the matter becomes even more simple.”* --- *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes — Arthur Conan Doyle* --- *Tags: Neutral, Written*
- **besides** --- apart from that; used to add an argument --- además, total --- *I don't want to go out; besides, it's raining.* --- *No quiero salir; además, está lloviendo.* --- *“Amy put a blue ribbon on the boy and a pink on the girl, French fashion, so you can always tell. Besides, one has blue eyes and one brown. Kiss them, Uncle Teddy,” said wicked Jo.* --- *Little Women — Louisa May Alcott* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken*
- **what's more** --- used to add something surprising or important --- y lo que es más, y encima --- *She passed the exam; what's more, she got the highest mark.* --- *Aprobó el examen; y lo que es más, sacó la nota más alta.* --- *The restaurant was cheap, and what's more, the food was excellent. We will certainly come back next Friday.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*
- **not only ... but also** --- used to add a second, stronger point --- no solo ... sino también --- *The hotel was not only cheap but also quiet.* --- *El hotel no solo era barato sino también tranquilo.* --- *“Don’t have a mother,” he said. Not only had he no mother, but he had not the slightest desire to have one. He thought them very over-rated persons.* --- *Peter Pan — J. M. Barrie* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **additionally** --- used to add a further point --- además, a su vez --- *The hotel is cheap. Additionally, it offers free parking.* --- *El hotel es barato. Además, ofrece estacionamiento gratuito.* --- *The hotel is cheap and clean. Additionally, the buses stop right outside every ten minutes.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **on top of that** --- used to add something even more annoying or impressive --- y a eso se le suma, y encima --- *The train was late; on top of that, we lost the reservation.* --- *El tren estaba atrasado; y encima, perdimos la reserva.* --- *The train was late and we lost our seats. On top of that, the hotel had lost our booking.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Informal, Spoken*

## Contrast and concession

- **however** --- used to introduce an opposite idea --- sin embargo, pero --- *The food was good; however, the service was slow.* --- *La comida estaba bien; sin embargo, el servicio fue lento.* --- *It will be uphill work, I expect, for Mrs. Thomas often told me I was desperately wicked. However, I’ll do my very best. But can you tell me why I’m crying?”* --- *Anne of Green Gables — Lucy Maud Montgomery* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **nevertheless** --- in spite of what has just been said --- no obstante, aun así --- *It rained all day; nevertheless, we went for a walk.* --- *Llovió todo el día; aun así, salimos a caminar.* --- *"A Merry Christmas and a Happy New Year to the old man, whatever he is!" said Scrooge's nephew. "He wouldn't take it from me, but may he have it, nevertheless. Uncle Scrooge!"* --- *A Christmas Carol — Charles Dickens* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **on the other hand** --- used to compare a different point --- por otro lado --- *This flat is cheap; on the other hand, it is far from work.* --- *Este departamento es barato; por otro lado, está lejos del trabajo.* --- *“It is of importance that it should not rain before we are able to go over the ground. On the other hand, a man should be at his very best and keenest for such nice work as that, and I did not wish to do it when fagged by a long journey.* --- *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes — Arthur Conan Doyle* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **although** --- in spite of the fact that --- aunque --- *Although it was cold, we went to the beach.* --- *Aunque hacía frío, fuimos a la playa.* --- *There, there, child, don’t cry. I can’t see as you were to blame although I’m sorry it happened so.” “I must cry,” said Anne.* --- *Anne of Green Gables — Lucy Maud Montgomery* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **even though** --- used to emphasise a contrast --- aunque, pese a que --- *Even though she was tired, she finished the report.* --- *Aunque estaba cansada, terminó el informe.* --- *"I don't know. But before that time we shall be ready with the money; and even though we were not, it would be a bad fortune indeed to find so merciless a creditor in his successor. We may sleep to-night with light hearts, Caroline!"* --- *A Christmas Carol — Charles Dickens* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **whereas** --- in contrast with the fact that --- mientras que, en cambio --- *Tom works at night, whereas Mia works in the morning.* --- *Tom trabaja de noche, mientras que Mia trabaja de mañana.* --- *In the South one man all by himself, has stopped a stage full of men in the daytime, and robbed the lot. Your newspapers call you a brave people so much that you think you are braver than any other people—whereas you’re just as brave, and no braver. Why don’t your juries hang murderers?* --- *Huckleberry Finn — Mark Twain* --- *Tags: Neutral, Written*
- **despite** --- used with a noun to show contrast --- a pesar de, pese a --- *Despite the rain, the match continued.* --- *A pesar de la lluvia, el partido siguió.* --- *“Stopped to get the license for tomorrow, ma’am.” “Which side won the last match, Teddy?” inquired Jo, who persisted in feeling an interest in manly sports despite her nineteen years.* --- *Little Women — Louisa May Alcott* --- *Tags: Neutral, Written*
- **in spite of** --- used with a noun to show contrast --- a pesar de, pese a --- *In spite of the noise, I fell asleep quickly.* --- *A pesar del ruido, me dormí rápido.* --- *So they kept walking until Dorothy could stand no longer. Her eyes closed in spite of herself and she forgot where she was and fell among the poppies, fast asleep. “What shall we do?” asked the Tin Woodman.* --- *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — L. Frank Baum* --- *Tags: Neutral, Written*

## Cause and reason

- **because** --- for the reason that --- porque, ya que --- *We stayed home because it was raining.* --- *Nos quedamos en casa porque estaba lloviendo.* --- *“You don’t want me!” she cried. “You don’t want me because I’m not a boy! I might have expected it.* --- *Anne of Green Gables — Lucy Maud Montgomery* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **since** --- because; used when the reason is already known --- ya que, puesto que --- *Since you are here, help me with this box.* --- *Ya que estás acá, ayúdame con esta caja.* --- *“You will now stand on the platform till recess,” said Mr. Davis, resolved to do the thing thoroughly, since he had begun. That was dreadful.* --- *Little Women — Louisa May Alcott* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **due to** --- caused by --- debido a, a causa de --- *The flight was cancelled due to the storm.* --- *El vuelo se canceló debido a la tormenta.* --- *She lifted me from the pole in the cornfield and brought me to the Emerald City. So my good luck is all due to her, and I shall never leave her until she starts back to Kansas for good and all.”* --- *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — L. Frank Baum* --- *Tags: Neutral, Written*
- **owing to** --- because of (more formal) --- debido a, por causa de --- *Owing to the heat, the match was moved to the evening.* --- *Debido al calor, el partido se trasladó a la tarde.* --- *He was ashamed of himself, and yet he did it. It was all owing to his too affectionate nature, which craved for admiration.* --- *Peter Pan — J. M. Barrie* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **on account of** --- because of --- debido a, por motivo de --- *We left early on account of the traffic.* --- *Salimos temprano debido al tráfico.* --- *“Oh, did you see the Premier?” exclaimed Anne at once. “What did he look like Marilla?” “Well, he never got to be Premier on account of his looks,” said Marilla.* --- *Anne of Green Gables — Lucy Maud Montgomery* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **for this reason** --- used to introduce the result of the preceding cause --- por esta razón, por eso --- *The bus never came; for this reason, we walked home.* --- *El colectivo nunca llegó; por esta razón, caminamos hasta casa.* --- *There are wild beasts in the woods, and a race of queer men who do not like strangers to cross their country. For this reason none of the Quadlings ever come to the Emerald City.”* --- *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — L. Frank Baum* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **as** --- because; used to give a reason --- ya que, porque --- *As it was late, we took a taxi.* --- *Como era tarde, tomamos un taxi.* --- *As it was already dark, we called a taxi. The last bus had left. We arrived home before midnight.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **given that** --- considering that --- dado que, teniendo en cuenta que --- *Given that it is Sunday, the bank is closed.* --- *Dado que es domingo, el banco está cerrado.* --- *Given that prices are rising every month, we should book the trip now. Tomorrow it will cost more.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Neutral, Written*

## Result and consequence

- **therefore** --- for that reason --- por lo tanto, entonces --- *The road was closed; therefore, we took the motorway.* --- *La ruta estaba cerrada; por lo tanto, tomamos la autopista.* --- *Now I growl when I’m pleased, and wag my tail when I’m angry. Therefore I’m mad.” “I call it purring, not growling,” said Alice. “Call it what you like,” said the Cat.* --- *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland — Lewis Carroll* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **thus** --- as a result (formal) --- así, por lo tanto --- *He saved every month; thus, he bought a car in a year.* --- *Ahorró cada mes; así, compró un auto en un año.* --- *They took it for granted that if they went he would go also, but really they scarcely cared. M. Barrie* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **consequently** --- as a result --- en consecuencia, por lo tanto --- *She missed the train; consequently, she arrived late.* --- *Perdió el tren; en consecuencia, llegó tarde.* --- *It was a gory day. Consequently it was an extremely satisfactory one. They assembled in camp toward suppertime, hungry and happy; but now a difficulty arose—hostile Indians could not break the bread of hospitality together without first making peace, and this was a simple impossibility without smoking a pipe of peace.* --- *Tom Sawyer — Mark Twain* --- *Tags: Formal, Written*
- **as a result** --- used to introduce the effect of something --- como resultado --- *It snowed a lot; as a result, the schools closed.* --- *Nevó mucho; como resultado, cerraron las escuelas.* --- *Diana’s mother had found out about the Haunted Wood and had been decidedly angry over it. - **hence** --- therefore (formal) --- por lo tanto, de ahí --- *The hotel is far; hence, we need a car.* --- *El hotel está lejos; por lo tanto, necesitamos un auto.* --- *Demand fell all year, and hence the factory closed early. - **accordingly** --- for that reason; in a suitable way --- en consecuencia, en conformidad --- *The plan changed; accordingly, we updated the calendar.* --- *El plan cambió; en consecuencia, actualizamos el calendario.* --- *“Look at my hair, Marilla,” she whispered. Accordingly, Marilla lifted her candle and looked scrutinizingly at Anne’s hair, flowing in heavy masses down her back. - **so** --- used to introduce a consequence --- así que, por lo tanto --- *The queue was long, so we left.* --- *La fila era larga, así que nos fuimos.* --- *The country here is rich and pleasant, but you must pass through rough and dangerous places before you reach the end of your journey.” This worried Dorothy a little, but she knew that only the Great Oz could help her get to Kansas again, so she bravely resolved not to turn back. - **as a consequence** --- used to introduce a result --- como consecuencia --- *He never cooked; as a consequence, he ate badly.* --- *Nunca cocinaba; como consecuencia, comía mal.* --- *He never cooked or cleaned. ## Purpose

- **so that** --- with the purpose that --- para que --- *Speak slowly so that everyone can understand.* --- *Hablá despacio para que todos puedan entender.* --- *I moved closer so that everyone could hear me. The room was noisy. Then we began the meeting.* --- *Everyday conversation* --- *Tags: Neutral, Spoken & written*
- **in order to** --- used before a verb to say why --- para, a fin de --- *We left early in order to catch the ferry.* --- *Salimos temprano para alcanzar el transbordador.* --- *“My name,” said he, “is John Openshaw, but my own affairs have, as far as I can understand, little to do with this awful business. - **in order that** --- used with a subject and a modal verb (formal) --- a fin de que --- *He spoke clearly in order that the class could follow.* --- *Habló claramente a fin de que la clase pudiera seguir.* --- *An inspection of his chair showed me that he had been in the habit of standing on it, which of course would be necessary in order that he should reach the ventilator. - **so as to** --- used before a verb; in order to --- para, a fin de --- *Please whisper so as not to wake the baby.* --- *Susurrá para no despertar al bebé.* --- *All I wanted was to go somewheres; all I wanted was a change, I warn’t particular. - **with the aim of** --- with the intention of --- con el objetivo de --- *The school opened a club with the aim of teaching cooking.* --- *La escuela abrió un club con el objetivo de enseñar a cocinar.* --- *He moved abroad with the aim of improving his English. ## Time and sequence

- **subsequently** --- after something else has happened --- posteriormente, después --- *The flight was delayed; subsequently, we missed the connection.* --- *El vuelo estaba atrasado; posteriormente, perdimos la conexión.* --- *We visited the museum in the morning. - **meanwhile** --- during the same period --- mientras tanto --- *I cooked; meanwhile, my brother set the table.* --- *Yo cocinaba; mientras tanto, mi hermano puso la mesa.* --- *So he stepped ashore and entered the woods. - **in the meantime** --- during the period before something happens --- mientras tanto, en el interín --- *The repairs take a week; in the meantime, we can use the other car.* --- *Las reparaciones llevan una semana; mientras tanto, podemos usar el otro auto.* --- *“Who said I wasn’t getting up?” she cried. ## Examples and clarification
